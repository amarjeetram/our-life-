## Resizing vs Compressing: The Real Difference

### Resizing
Resizing changes the **physical dimensions** of an image — its width and height in pixels.

Example: A photo that is 4000 × 3000 pixels → resized to 400 × 300 pixels.

Resizing WILL reduce file size (fewer pixels = less data to store), but you cannot predict the exact KB outcome. A resized 400×300 photo might be 50 KB or 500 KB depending on the original quality.

### Compressing
Compressing reduces the **file weight** of an image without necessarily changing dimensions.

Example: A 4000 × 3000 pixel photo at 4 MB → compressed to the same 4000 × 3000 pixels but only 100 KB.

Compression removes invisible data (nearly identical color values) that your eyes cannot perceive, making the file lighter while keeping the dimensions the same.

### What "Resize to 100 KB" Really Means
When a portal says this, they almost always mean: **reduce the file size to under 100 KB** — which is compression. The dimensions are a secondary concern (usually specified separately as "must be at least 200×230 pixels").

Use compression to hit the 100 KB target. Use resizing separately if your dimensions are also outside the required range.
