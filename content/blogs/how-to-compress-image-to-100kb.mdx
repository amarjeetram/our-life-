## Why Do Websites Ask for 100KB Photos?

The 100 KB limit exists for very practical reasons:

**Server Storage:** Popular job portals and educational websites receive thousands of photo uploads every day. If each photo was 5 MB, the servers would fill up incredibly fast. At 100 KB, they can store 50 times more photos for the same storage cost.

**Upload Speed:** Not everyone has fast internet. A 100 KB file uploads in under 1 second on even a slow 3G connection. A 5 MB file could take several minutes, causing the page to time out.

**Processing Speed:** When an HR manager or government officer opens your application, your photo needs to load instantly. Tiny files load in milliseconds.

The 100 KB limit is generally used in situations where a slightly higher quality photo is acceptable — such as professional job applications where an HR officer needs to see your face clearly.

## Tips for Great Results

### Take a Good Original Photo
The quality of your compressed photo depends heavily on the quality of your original. Take your photo:
- In **bright natural light** (near a window, not in a dark room)
- Against a **plain white or light background**
- With both hands **holding the phone steady**
- From a **straight-on angle** (not from below or above)

### Use JPG Format
JPG (also called JPEG) is the best format for photographs. It compresses much better than PNG. Most government and corporate portals specifically request JPG format. If you have a PNG photo, our tool will automatically convert it to an optimized JPG.

### Don't Over-Compress
If a portal allows up to 100 KB, do not feel you need to compress to 20 KB. A 85-90 KB photo will look better than a 15 KB photo while still passing the upload check. Compress to the right target, not smaller than needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I compressed to 100KB but the portal still says "file too large." Why?
Double-check: some portals are actually asking for 100 KB for the photo AND a separate smaller size for the signature. Make sure you're compressing the right file. Also verify the number — some portals say 100 KB but mean 100×100 pixels (dimensions, not file size).

### Can I compress a photo that's already compressed?
Yes, but results may vary. Each round of lossy compression removes a bit more quality. If you're starting from an already-compressed 200 KB photo, the result at 100 KB will be good. But if you start from a fresh, high-quality 5 MB original, the 100 KB result will look much better.

### My phone won't let me access the downloaded photo. What should I do?
On Android, the downloaded file should appear in your Downloads folder and in your Gallery. On iPhone, it should appear in your Photos library. If you can't find it, check your Downloads folder directly using a file manager app.
