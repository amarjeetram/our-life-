## What Does "Compress to 50KB" Actually Mean?

First, let us understand what is happening. Every photo on your phone or computer has a "weight." This weight is called the **file size**. File size is measured in units:

- **KB (Kilobyte)** = Very light. Like a feather.
- **MB (Megabyte)** = Very heavy. Like a brick.

Your phone's camera is very smart and takes very detailed pictures. Because details take space, each picture becomes very heavy — usually 2 MB, 3 MB, or even 5 MB.

But websites, especially government and job portals, have strict rules. They only want **light** photos because:
1. Their computers don't have unlimited storage
2. Heavy photos take longer to process
3. They want to be fair to people with slow internet

So when they say "Max 50 KB," they mean your photo must weigh **50 Kilobytes or less**.

## Why Can't I Just Send My Big Photo?

Here is the honest answer: our tool uses **smart lossy compression**. This is a very clever type of math that works like this:

## Tips for the Best Result

To get the best quality photo even after compression, follow these tips:

1. **Take the original photo in good light.** Stand near a bright window. Avoid dark rooms or harsh shadows on your face.
2. **Use a plain background.** Stand against a white or light-colored wall. A plain background compresses better and makes your face clearer.
3. **Hold the phone steady.** A blurry original photo will become even blurrier after compression. Use two hands. Hold your breath while pressing the shutter.
4. **Use JPG format.** JPG photos compress much better than PNG photos. If you have a PNG, our tool can handle it, but JPG is always the best starting point.
