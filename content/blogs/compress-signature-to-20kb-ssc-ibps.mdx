When you register for SSC (Staff Selection Commission) or IBPS (Institute of Banking Personnel Selection) exams, you must upload two things: your passport photo and your signature. While the photo gets all the attention, the signature upload causes almost as many headaches.

The requirement is usually strict: **Signature file must be between 10 KB and 20 KB in JPG format.**

This guide will teach you exactly how to take a perfect signature photo and compress it to the required 20 KB size.





## Why Signatures Have a Smaller Size Limit

Signatures require a smaller file size than passport photos for a simple reason: they contain less visual information.

A face photo has thousands of colors — skin tones, eyes, hair, clothing, background gradients. All of these colors require data to store.

A signature, on the other hand, is essentially **two colors**: dark ink and white paper. This makes signatures compress extremely efficiently. A high-quality signature photo can easily reach 15 KB without looking any different from the original.

## SSC and IBPS Photo/Signature Requirements

| Document | Size Range | Format | Background |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passport Photo | 20 KB – 50 KB | JPG | White |
| Signature | 10 KB – 20 KB | JPG | White |
| Thumb Impression | 10 KB – 20 KB | JPG | White |

The most common mistake students make is uploading a beautifully compressed passport photo but then uploading a raw, uncompressed 500 KB signature! Both documents have separate size limits.

## Step 1: Take the Perfect Signature Photo

### Materials Needed
- A clean white A4 sheet of paper
- A blue or black ballpoint pen (Reynolds, Cello, or similar)
- Your smartphone
- Good natural lighting

### Process

**Prepare the environment:**
Place the white paper on a flat, clean table surface. Position yourself near a window so daylight falls evenly on the paper.

**Sign the paper:**
Write your signature in the center of the paper. Make it slightly larger than your usual signature for better visibility. Use consistent pen pressure — neither too light (lines will be faint) nor too heavy (lines will be too thick and could bleed).

**Photograph the signature:**
Hold your phone directly above the paper — look straight down at it. The phone should be parallel to the paper, not tilted at any angle. Tap on the signature on your phone screen to focus the camera precisely on it. Take the photo.

**Verify the photo:**
Open the photo in your Gallery. Zoom in 2-3x. The individual ink lines of your signature should be sharp, dark, and smooth — not blurry or jagged.

## Step 2: Crop the Signature Photo

Before compressing, crop the photo to show only the signature area.

**Why crop?**
If you took the photo of the entire A4 page, most of the image is empty white space. That white space adds to the file size without adding useful information. Cropping to just the signature area reduces the file size and makes the signature fill the upload frame properly.

**How to crop on Android:**
Gallery → Open photo → Edit (pencil icon) → Crop → Drag handles to surround just the signature with a small white border → Save copy

**How to crop on iPhone:**
Photos → Open photo → Edit → Crop (square icon) → Adjust to surround signature → Done

The cropped signature should have your signature centered with about 10-15% white space around all four sides.

## Why Signatures Sometimes Look Faded After Compression

The most common problem: you compress your signature and the lines look grey and thin instead of dark blue.

**Usually caused by:**
- Original photo taken in artificial yellow light (gives a warm tone that makes blue ink look pale)
- Original photo slightly out of focus
- Pens with very thin or light ink (like a pencil-style 0.5mm ballpoint)

**Solutions:**
1. Move to a window with natural daylight — this makes blue ink look crisp and dark
2. Make sure the camera is focused on the signature, not the table
3. Use a thick, dark ballpoint pen with generous ink (avoid light pens)

## Thumb Impression Upload for Competitive Exams

Some competitive exam portals also ask for a **left thumb impression** in addition to signature. The same size rule applies: 10 KB to 20 KB in JPG.

**How to take a thumb impression photo:**
1. Press your left thumb firmly onto a blue/black ink pad
2. Press your inked thumb onto clean white paper
3. Let it dry completely (30 seconds)
4. Photograph it from directly above in natural light
5. Crop to just the thumb impression area
6. Compress to 15-18 KB using our tool

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a digital signature app on my phone instead of photographing a paper signature?
Possibly, but this depends on the exam portal. Most SSC and IBPS portals require a photograph of a handwritten signature on paper. Digital drawing app signatures are sometimes rejected. When in doubt, use the traditional paper method.

### My signature upload was rejected because "both lines of text do not match." What does this mean?
Some portals check that your signature (written in the box) and your name (typed in the form) are visually consistent. Make sure your signature represents your actual legal name or the form's instruction regarding what the signature should say.

### I'm left-handed. Should I sign with my right hand for official documents?
No — always use your dominant hand. Sign the way you naturally sign any document. If you are left-handed, sign with your left hand.
