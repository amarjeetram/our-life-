## Understanding 30 KB

Let's start with the basics. A kilobyte (KB) is a unit for measuring digital file size.

A smartphone photo is typically **3,000 KB to 8,000 KB** (3 to 8 MB). The 30 KB limit means you need to reduce your photo's weight by about **100 to 250 times**.

This sounds extreme, but modern compression technology handles it beautifully. A 30 KB photo is still large enough to clearly show your face, eyes, and basic features — everything needed for government record-keeping and identity verification.

## Why Railway and State Exam Portals Use 30 KB Limits

Railway recruitment portals receive applications in the **millions**. During RRB NTPC or Group D notifications, the server often handles over 1 crore (10 million) applications in a few weeks.

With 1 crore applicants each uploading a photo and a signature, even at 30 KB each, that's still 60 GB of data (1 crore × 30 KB for photo + 1 crore × 15 KB for signature). Imagine if each photo was 5 MB — that would be 10 terabytes of data just for photos! Completely unmanageable.

The 30 KB limit allows the system to handle millions of applications smoothly without crashing.

## Tips for the Best 30 KB Compression Result

**Tip 1: Dont use a dark room.** Photos taken in poor lighting compress badly. The shadows and noise in a dark photo require extra data to store, making it harder to reach 30 KB without visible degradation. Always use natural daylight.

**Tip 2: Use a solid white background.** A complex background (textured wall, furniture, other people) makes the photo larger and harder to compress cleanly. A plain white wall gives the cleanest compression results.

**Tip 3: Face the light.** Position yourself so the light from the window hits your face from the front. Dont stand with your back to the window (this creates a silhouette effect).

**Tip 4: Check your original is focused.** Before compressing, zoom in to 100% on your original photo. Are your eyes sharp and in focus? If the original is blurry, no compression tool can fix that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### The RRB notification says "20 KB to 50 KB" but I want to be safe. Is 30 KB a good target?
Yes! 30 KB sits comfortably in the middle of a 20-50 KB range. It gives you excellent quality while staying well within the limits. Our tool will target just under 30 KB.

### My original photo is already a JPG. Does that mean it's small enough?
Not necessarily. JPG is just the format, not the size. A JPG photo from your phone camera is still typically 3-5 MB (3,000-5,000 KB). You still need to compress it to 30 KB using our tool.

### I live in a village without stable internet. Can I compress offline?
For offline compression, you would need to download a phone app with compression capabilities. However, the best results come from online tools. If you have even a 2G connection, the upload process (typically 3-5 MB) should be manageable by doing it slowly. Try using a cyber cafe or a location with better signal.

### My photo passed the 30 KB size check but the portal said "Face not detected." What happened?
Some modern portals use AI to detect faces in uploaded photos. This error usually means:
1. Your face was not centered enough in the photo
2. The photo is too blurry to detect a face
3. You were wearing dark glasses or a face mask

Retake the photo with a clear, forward-facing, unobstructed face and try again.
