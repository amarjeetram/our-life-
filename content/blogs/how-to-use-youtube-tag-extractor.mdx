## What Are YouTube Tags?

When you upload a video to YouTube, you can add tags to it. Tags are short keywords or phrases that describe your video's content.

For example, if you upload a cooking video about "How to Make Biryani," you might add tags like:
- biryani recipe
- how to make biryani
- chicken biryani
- Indian cooking
- rice recipes
- biryani at home

YouTube reads these tags to understand:
1. **What is this video about?** The tags help YouTube categorize your content
2. **Who should see this video?** Tags help match your video to the right search queries
3. **What related videos should appear next?** Videos with similar tags appear in the "Up next" section

## What to Do With Extracted Tags

Once you have a list of tags from successful videos:

### Create Your Tag List
YouTube allows up to **500 characters** of tags. That's space for roughly 15 to 25 tags depending on length.

Build your tag list with a mix:
- 3-5 **broad tags** (the main topic): "biryani recipe," "Indian food"
- 5-8 **specific tags** (the video angle): "chicken biryani home recipe," "restaurant style biryani"
- 3-5 **long-tail tags** (very specific phrases): "how to make biryani at home without dum"
- 3-5 **related topic tags** (similar content): "rice dishes," "Indian dinner recipes"

### Order Your Tags Wisely
Put your most important, specific tags first. YouTube gives slightly more weight to the first few tags.

### Don't Keyword Stuff
Using 100 irrelevant tags thinking "more is better" is a mistake. YouTube may penalize videos for tag stuffing — using tags that have nothing to do with the video. Only use tags that are genuinely relevant.

## Tags vs. Title vs. Description: What Matters More?

Many new YouTubers think tags are the most important ranking factor. In reality:

**Most important:** Video title, thumbnail click-through rate, watch time, audience retention
**Very important:** Video description (first 2-3 lines)
**Important (but less than before):** Tags

Tags still matter, especially for discovery through related videos and some search queries. But they work best when they complement a strong title and engaging content that people actually watch.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I extract my own video's tags to check them?
Yes! Paste your own video URL into the extractor. It's a great way to audit your existing tags and compare them to competing videos.

### Why does the tool show zero tags for some videos?
Not all creators use tags. Some (especially large channels) rely primarily on titles, descriptions, and YouTube's AI to rank their videos. If a popular video has zero tags, it means the creator chose not to add them.

### Are tags visible to normal YouTube viewers?
No. Regular viewers watching on YouTube do not see tags. Tags are only visible in the video's backend settings (for the creator) or through tools like ours that read the page's HTML code.

### How often should I update my video tags?
If a video is performing well, don't change its tags — you might disrupt what's working. If a video is underperforming, consider updating tags based on new keyword research or competitor analysis.
