SSC, RRB, and state government exam portals typically require TWO uploads from candidates: a passport photo AND a signature. While the passport photo gets most attention, the signature upload has its own strict requirements — usually between **10 KB and 30 KB**.

This guide focuses specifically on signature compression to 30 KB, covering everything from how to take a perfect signature photo to ensuring the compressed result will be accepted.





## Why Signature Photos Have a Smaller Size Limit

Unlike a face photo that contains thousands of different colors (skin tones, hair, background), a signature is essentially:
- **Dark ink lines** on **white paper**

This simplicity makes signature photos compress extremely efficiently. A high-quality signature photo can comfortably reach 15-25 KB with zero visible quality loss.

The 30 KB limit for signatures isn't just a technical constraint — it's actually quite generous for the content type. Even with moderate compression, signatures look excellent.

## The Perfect Signature: What Feels Right Looks Right

Before worrying about compression, understand what a good digital signature looks like:

**Lines must be dark.** A faint signature in pencil or a light pen will look terrible when compressed. Use a blue or black ballpoint pen with good ink flow.

**Background must be pure white.** Lined paper, graph paper, or yellow notepad paper will be visible in the photo and could cause the portal to reject the upload.

**Size should be substantial.** Sign larger than your normal everyday signature — perhaps 1.5x to 2x your usual size. Larger original signatures compress more cleanly than tiny, delicate ones.

**Consistent ink flow.** Press the pen firmly and consistently throughout the signature. Avoid spots where the line is very thick at the start and then trails off thin.

## Step 1: Creating the Perfect Signature Photo

### Materials
- White A4 sheet (no lines, no patterns)
- Blue ballpoint pen (Reynolds 045 or similar — medium weight)
- Bright natural light from a window
- Your smartphone

### Process

1. Place the white paper on a clean, flat surface. Put it on a table near a window.

2. Sign your name with full, bold strokes. Use more pen pressure than your usual writing. The lines should be clearly visible from 1 meter away.

3. Let the ink dry completely — 30 seconds is enough.

4. Position your phone directly ABOVE the paper, looking straight down. The phone should be parallel to the paper, not at an angle.

5. In your camera, tap on the signature to focus precisely on it. Do not use flash.

6. Take 5-6 photos. Choose the one where the ink looks darkest and the lines are sharpest.

## Step 2: Crop the Signature Photo

In your phone's Gallery:
1. Open the chosen signature photo
2. Tap Edit → Crop
3. Drag the crop handles to surround just your signature, leaving about 10-15% white space on all four sides
4. Save the cropped version

This cropping step is important — it removes the surrounding blank paper that adds unnecessary file size.

## What If Your Signature Looks Slightly Faded?

If the compressed signature looks lighter than expected — the ink lines appear grey instead of dark blue — the issue is almost always the original photo, not the compression.

Most common causes:
- **Yellow artificial light** (from ceiling bulbs) → Makes blue ink look pale/yellow
- **Shadows** across part of the signature → Uneven appearance
- **Used a pencil or light pen** → Lines don't have enough contrast

Fix: Retake the photo in bright natural window light. This makes blue ballpoint ink look crisp and dark, even after significant compression.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I updated my signature style since last year. Should I use the old or new signature?
Use your CURRENT natural signature consistently. Government ID verification compares your current signature to the one on record. Drastic changes can cause issues. If your signature has evolved, use your current version going forward.

### The portal shows a preview of my signature after upload and it looks pixelated. Is that a problem?
Portal previews are often very small thumbnails (50-100 pixels wide). They always look pixelated at thumbnail size. What matters is that the actual file (15-25 KB JPG) looks clear when you open it at full size on your device.

### Can I photograph my signature using the front camera?
Better to use the rear camera — it's higher quality on almost all phones. Use the timer or landscape mode with screen facing down, or ask a friend to take the photo.
