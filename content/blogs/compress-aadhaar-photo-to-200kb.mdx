Your Aadhaar card is India's most important identity document. Millions of Indians upload photos or scans of their Aadhaar card for bank account opening, mobile SIM verification, government services, and scholarship applications.

But a scanned or photographed Aadhaar card is often a large file — sometimes 2 MB, 5 MB, or even larger. Many portals that ask for your Aadhaar copy limit the file size to **200 KB or less**.

This guide shows you how to safely compress your Aadhaar photo to under 200 KB while keeping every detail — your name, Aadhaar number, address, photo, and QR code — perfectly readable.





## When You Need to Upload an Aadhaar Photo at 200 KB

The most common situations:

**Bank Account Opening:** Most banks in India now offer online zero-balance account opening via video KYC. They ask you to upload a photo of your Aadhaar card for address and identity proof. File size limits vary from 100 KB to 500 KB.

**Scholarship Applications:** State scholarship portals (NSP, state-level portals) require Aadhaar upload as income and identity proof. Many cap the file size at 200 KB.

**SIM Card Verification:** While most SIM verification now happens via real-time Aadhaar authentication, some portals still ask for an Aadhaar image at under 200 KB for documentation.

**Government Schemes:** Various government benefit schemes (PM-KISAN, housing schemes, etc.) require Aadhaar upload for eligibility verification.

## How to Take a Good Aadhaar Card Photo

A blurry or poorly lit Aadhaar photo will look even worse when compressed. Getting the original right is crucial.

### Method 1: Photograph With Your Smartphone

1. Place your Aadhaar card on a flat, clean surface
2. Position yourself directly above the card (bird's eye view, not at an angle)
3. Make sure the card is near a window with natural daylight
4. Do NOT use flash — it creates glare and makes the text impossible to read
5. Tap the camera screen to focus precisely on the Aadhaar card
6. Take the photo and zoom in to check: can you read all text clearly?

**Common mistakes:**
- Taking at an angle (card becomes a parallelogram, text is distorted)
- Using flash (glare hides text)
- Taking with only one hand (blurry photo)
- Too much shadow from leaning over the card

### Method 2: Use a Scanner (Best Option If Available)

If you have access to a flatbed scanner or a phone scanner app (like Adobe Scan, Microsoft Lens, or Google PhotoScan), scanning gives much sharper results than photography.

Scan at **200-300 DPI** for an excellent result that compresses cleanly to 200 KB.

## What If the Portal Says "Image Is Not Readable/Clear"?

This happens when the compressed image doesn't have enough resolution for the portal's automatic text recognition (OCR) system to read the Aadhaar number or other text.

**Fix 1:** Retake the original photo with more attention to focus and lighting. A sharper original will compress better.

**Fix 2:** If possible, use a higher target file size. If the portal allows up to 500 KB, don't compress all the way to 200 KB — try 400 KB.

**Fix 3:** Use a document scanner app instead of a phone camera. Scanner apps process the image to enhance text clarity specifically.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I upload a screenshot of my Aadhaar app as the document?
Some portals accept a screenshot from the mAadhaar app or the UIDAI website. However, many portals require a physical document scan or photo. Always check the portal's specific upload guidelines.

### The portal is asking for PDF format, not image. What should I do?
If the portal asks for a PDF, you'll need to convert your compressed image to PDF format. Many online tools can convert JPG to PDF. Our tool outputs compressed images as JPG, which you can then convert to PDF using a free conversion tool if needed.

### Is 200 KB large enough to show all the text on my Aadhaar card?
