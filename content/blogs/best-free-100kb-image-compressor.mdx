## Tips for Getting the Best 100KB Compression Result

Regardless of which tool you use, these tips will help you get the best possible result:

### Start with a High-Quality Original
The better your original photo, the better the compressed result will be. Take photos in bright natural light, with steady hands, against a plain background.

### Don't Over-Compress
If the portal allows up to 100 KB, aim for 80-95 KB. Don't try to compress to 20 KB unless the portal requires it. The closer you are to the maximum allowed size, the better your photo quality will be.

### Use JPG Format
Start with a JPG photo if possible. JPG is designed for photographs and compresses far better than PNG. If you only have a PNG, the tool will convert it, but JPG originals give the best results.

### Compress Fresh Each Time
If you compressed a photo a week ago and need to change the output, go back to the original high-quality photo and compress from scratch. Don't compress the already-compressed version.

### Check the Result on Different Screens
After downloading, open the photo on both your phone and, if possible, a computer screen. Make sure it looks clear on both. What looks fine on a phone screen might look slightly different on a large monitor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are all online image compressors safe to use?
Not all of them. The key things to check are: (1) Does the website use HTTPS? (2) Does the privacy policy state photos are deleted after processing? (3) Is it a known, trusted tool? For sensitive documents like ID cards and face photos, use only well-reviewed tools with clear privacy policies.

### What if my compressed photo passes the size limit but the portal says "invalid image"?
This usually means the portal has dimension (pixel width and height) requirements in addition to file size requirements. Check the portal's upload guidelines for dimension specs. If needed, resize to the correct dimensions first, then compress.

### Can I trust the quality rating or score some tools give?
Quality scores like "85%" are relative to the comparison. A photo with 85% quality score still looks excellent to human eyes. These numbers are just technical measurements — what matters is whether your face and documents look clear when you (and the examiner) look at the photo.
